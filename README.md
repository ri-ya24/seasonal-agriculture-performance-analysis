# Seasonal Agriculture Performance Analysis

##  Project Overview

This project presents a data-driven analysis of seasonal agricultural performance across different crops, geographical regions, farming conditions, resource usage, and economic outcomes.

The analysis focuses on identifying seasonal patterns, variations, relationships, and unusual agricultural conditions using exploratory data analysis, statistical techniques, correlation analysis, and visualization.

##  Objectives

- Explore and understand the agricultural dataset
- Clean and prepare the data for analysis
- Examine agricultural performance across Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid seasons
- Analyze crop yield, production, resource usage, and profitability
- Study relationships between environmental conditions and agricultural outcomes
- Compare agricultural performance across states and crop types
- Analyze water usage and water efficiency
- Identify unusual and high-risk agricultural patterns
- Apply appropriate statistical and visualization techniques
- Generate evidence-based insights and recommendations

##  Dataset

##  Key Findings

- **Kharif** shows the strongest overall seasonal performance in the dataset.
- **Zaid** records comparatively lower average yield, production, water efficiency, and profitability.
- **Sugarcane** demonstrates exceptionally high average yield, water efficiency, and profitability.
- Higher water consumption does not necessarily result in higher water efficiency or profitability.
- Production and yield show positive relationships with profitability.
- Geographical differences are visible across states, with **Punjab** showing the strongest overall average performance.
- Several high-risk observations combine high water usage, low water efficiency, and negative profitability.
- Seasonal differences in profitability are statistically significant, while seasonal differences in average yield are not statistically significant.

##  Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the project recommends:

- Improving water-use efficiency
- Using season-specific agricultural planning
- Considering crop profitability along with yield
- Monitoring high water-use and low-efficiency cases
- Using geographical performance for targeted planning
- Evaluating production, revenue, costs, and profit together
- Supporting agricultural decisions through data-driven analysis

##  Limitations

- The dataset covers selected states, districts, crops, and seasons.
- Observations are not equally distributed across all categories.
- Some missing numerical values were handled using median imputation.
- Correlation does not imply causation.
- Potential outliers were identified but not automatically removed because they may represent genuine agricultural conditions.
- Several real-world agricultural factors are not represented in the dataset.
